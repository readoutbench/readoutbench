## Summary
Radiation-induced lung injury (RILI) is one of the most common thoracic-radiotherapy complications, with an incidence as high as 31.4 %. Multiple studies have shown that RILI can adversely affect patient prognosis by disrupting treatment schedules. Moreover, the widespread clinical use of immune-checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has further increased pulmonary toxicity when radiotherapy (RT) is combined with ICIs. Checkpoint-inhibitor-related pneumonitis (CIP)-i.e., immune-mediated lung injury-may necessitate permanent discontinuation of ICIs, diminish survival benefit, and, in severe cases, directly threaten life. The diagnosis of both RILI and CIP is based on an integrated assessment of subjective symptoms and imaging findings.RILI typically occurs 1-3 months after completion of radiotherapy, whereas CIP may emerge at any point during treatment. The two entities share similar clinical presentations: fever, dry cough, chest tightness, dyspnoea, and pleuritic chest pain. Computed tomography (CT) is the most sensitive imaging modality. Pulmonary-function testing is another routinely used clinical metric; vital capacity, total lung capacity, forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV₁), and diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO) may all decline, with DLCO being the most sensitive parameter.
