# ReadoutBench Round 1: forecast 653 company trials before they report

These are the rules of Round 1 as it opens on 1 November 2026. They were revised through many rounds of outside review before launch, by AI review agents run independently of the build. The additions of 29 to 31 October (three deadlines, a ranked number score, and the safety, key secondary, subgroup, next-step and amendment questions) were checked by independent agents that wrote none of them, and their mechanics by the later outside AI reviews, which did not re-examine the design of these questions; the code that applies them, every results check and the build are published in the round's GitHub repository (https://github.com/readoutbench/readoutbench).

Round 1 of ReadoutBench has 653 trials. Each is a phase 2 or phase 3 trial registered on ClinicalTrials.gov as randomized, with these properties (548 of them have an efficacy or equivalence comparison the round scores; the other 105 do not, for example doses compared with no control or a design that is in fact single-arm, and are asked operations questions only):

- run by a company (not an individual sponsor-investigator or a non-profit, whatever the registry's sponsor class says);
- from any therapeutic area;
- primary completion, as registered, between July 2026 and March 2027;
- not stopped, and no public result of its primary outcome found on the launch date.

It is a prospective benchmark of forecasts of how these trials read out: the trials are a selected cohort, and only those that report by the deadline are scored for `p_met`, so the scores describe forecasting this cohort's readouts, not clinical-trial forecasting in general. There is no human-expert arm in Round 1.

The trials ask these kinds of questions:

| Trials | Will it work? (`p_met`, at three deadlines) | Numbers (`control`, `effect`; `subgroups`) | Safety, key secondary, next step | Will a result be out? (`p_public`, at three deadlines) | Operations (`p_late`, `p_stopped`, `p_posted`, `p_amended`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 536 superiority and non-inferiority trials | yes | 509 of them; subgroups on 275 phase 3 trials | yes (key secondary where the record has one) | yes | yes |
| 12 equivalence trials (biosimilars, bioequivalence, lot consistency) | yes: equivalence shown | no | next step only | yes | yes |
| 105 trials with no efficacy goal to meet (safety only, pilot, descriptive, no control group) | no | no | no | no | yes |

There are two boards. **Models**: one model, answering from the packet alone with the round's instructions unchanged; it compares models. **Open**: anything else, any harness, tools, web access or several models; it shows how far a system can go.

There are two rankings, both in tiers against a fixed reference, set once the first pass's labels are complete: `p_met` against the registry model (B1), and the number score against the frozen Opus reference entry. Everything else is shown on each entry's scorecard and never ranked.

Each trial comes with three files:

- its packet: the ClinicalTrials.gov record as pulled for the round (`packets/`; the pull date is in the packet), followed by its question card and its operations questions;
- its question card (`cards.jsonl`), which fixes the endpoint, unit, arms, design and planned arm sizes the questions refer to;
- its operations row (`operations.jsonl`): the status and registered primary completion date on the launch date, and which operations questions it asks.

`sisters.json` lists the sister-trial groups (trials of the same experimental drug) that the scoring resamples together. `forecast_instructions.md` is the round's prompt, the one the Models board uses. `manifest.json` holds the SHA-256 of every packet, of the cards, of the operations rows, of the sister groups and of the instructions. Before the round opens, the organiser posts the manifest's SHA-256 and the reference entries' SHA-256s outside the submission page, where it is dated and public: the round's GitHub repository (`fingerprints/2026-r1.txt`, and the release `2026-r1-fingerprints` at https://github.com/readoutbench/readoutbench/releases) (see Reference entries), and while the round is open posts a fingerprint of the list of uploads every week and at the close.

## What to forecast

For every trial whose card asks it (548 trials):

1. **`p_met`**: the probability that the trial meets its primary objective, **given that a result is public by the deadline (30 June 2027)**. Only trials that report by the deadline are scored, so this is the question the score asks; trials that report tend to be the ones that worked, and a forecast that knows this is a better forecast. "Met" means the sponsor or investigators report the trial's own primary hypothesis met: a statistically significant improvement, or non-inferiority against the trial's own pre-specified margin when it was designed to show it. If the card's design differs from the trial's own hypothesis, the trial's hypothesis decides; 16 of the 24 non-inferiority cards infer the design because the record does not state it, and say so (`design_basis`). For the 12 equivalence trials (card design `equivalence`), it is equivalence shown on the primary outcomes as the protocol defines it. An interim analysis that declares the objective met counts. "Met" follows the rules below. This is the headline question. It measures **reported success under the round's labelling rules**: a trial whose sponsor, by the deadline, only calls the result positive or encouraging counts as not met (rule 6). That is a rule about what was reported, not proof that the trial failed; the leaderboard shows how many scored trials were only vague, and every entry's score without them.

   **`p_met_mar`** and **`p_met_dec`** (optional): the same probability, given that a result is public by 31 March 2027, and by 31 December 2027: the early and final passes (see Dates). The three may differ: under rule 6, a trial still only vague at an earlier deadline counts as not met there, which is why the base rate for 31 March is a little below the one for 30 June (see Reference entries). Where an entry leaves either out for a trial, its `p_met` is used for that pass, and the share of trials where that happened is shown with the entry. The ranked result uses `p_met` alone.

For the trials whose card says `"numbers": "yes"` (the number questions), also give:

2. **`control`**: what the control arm will report, in the card's `control_measure` and `unit`, as your median and an 80% range (`low80` and `high80` are your 10th and 90th percentiles). Examples: median overall survival in months, the % of placebo patients responding, the mean change on placebo.
3. **`effect`**: the effect the trial will report, in the card's `effect_measure`, as a median and an 80% range in the same way:
   - a hazard ratio (or rate ratio) for time-to-event outcomes, as the ratio itself, for example 0.75;
   - the difference in percentage points, experimental minus control, for responder outcomes;
   - the difference in means, experimental minus control, in the outcome's unit, for continuous outcomes.

   The range is for the number the trial will publish, so it includes the trial's own chance variation.

   Both are asked **given that the trial reports after the round closes and publishes the number by the deadline of the pass that scores it** (30 June 2027 for the ranking). A number published later is not scored in that pass, and whether and when a number is published can depend on the result, as with `p_met`. The early and final passes reuse the same answers under their own deadlines and are shown only.

   The 44 cards whose record does not fully state the measure (its unit, time point, scale or form, such as absolute or percent change) say so (`measure_basis`: `assumed`), and their notes give the reading the card takes. The number questions refer to that reading; a result published only in another form leaves the numbers void, and `p_met` is not affected.

Optional, for the phase 3 trials with number questions whose card lists `subgroups` (279 trials; sex on the 259 that enrol both sexes, and on each one or two more subgroups chosen for the trial on 29 and 30 October):

- **`subgroups`**: the same effect within each group of each subgroup the card lists, as `{"<subgroup>": {"<group>": {"median", "low80", "high80"}, "<group>": {…}}, …}`, with the card's keys, in the card's effect measure. Each subgroup splits the patients into two groups, with the cut-off the field usually reports: a stratification factor named in the record where there is one, else a subgroup the record says it will analyse, else one that results in that disease are usually broken down by (age, region, baseline severity, prior therapy, a biomarker). They were chosen from the record alone by agents that did not see any forecast, and checked by others. Subgroup results usually come with the full presentation or paper, so many will resolve late or not at all; unresolved ones are void. A group resolves only when a publication reports exactly that group. Sex and the subgroups the record gives (a stratification factor, or one it says it will analyse) make the subgroup score; the ones chosen as commonly reported in the disease, most of them, are scored apart and shown as experimental, because a publication may well cut them differently. Shown, never ranked.

Optional, for the 536 superiority and non-inferiority trials:

- **`safety`**: the % of patients in the card's experimental arm and in its control arm who stop the study treatment (any part of it, for good) because of an adverse event, as `{"experimental": {"median", "low80", "high80"}, "control": {…}}`, each from 0 to 100. It resolves on the first source by the deadline of the pass (a press release, congress or paper; not the registry's posted results) that gives the share stopping any study treatment for an adverse event; if none does, the share stopping the experimental drug or its placebo; if none, the share leaving the study for an adverse event. For an arm with nothing to stop (surveillance, no treatment, delayed treatment, or a single dose given at the start), it is the share leaving the study for an adverse event. Void when none of these is published. Shown, never ranked.
- **`p_key_secondary`**, for the trials whose card names a key secondary endpoint (the one the record names as key or first in the testing order, else the first efficacy secondary outcome it lists that is not the primary repeated: at another time, in another form (percent instead of absolute change, a count of events instead of the time to the first), by another assessor, in another analysis set or in a subset; a responder analysis of the primary's measure, the share of patients reaching a threshold such as a 50% reduction, counts as an endpoint of its own; chosen on 29 and 30 October and checked by others): the probability that it is met, given that its result is public by the deadline. **Met**: a source reports it significant in favour of the experimental arm (p below the trial's level, two-sided 0.05 if none is stated, or a 95% interval that excludes no effect) and does not say it was outside formal testing. **Not met**: any other reported result, including one reported as not significant, as not formally tested (the testing order stopped, the primary was not met, or it was outside the testing plan), or without a test. **Void**: no result public by the deadline, or a result reported only as immature with a later formal analysis planned. A benefit means superiority, on non-inferiority cards too. Shown, never ranked.

Optional, for the 548 trials with `p_met`:

- **`p_next_step`**: the probability that, after the launch day and by 31 December 2027, the sponsor or a partner takes the next development step for the experimental drug, whatever this trial's result. For a phase 3 trial (phase 2/3 included): a marketing application, new or supplemental, in any country, that rests at least in part on this trial, announced as submitted, accepted for review or approved; the announcement must name this trial among the application's bases, or the sponsor must have said this trial supports it. For a phase 2 trial: such a marketing application, or a phase 3 trial of the drug in the same disease (a condition listed in this trial's record, at any stage or line of therapy) registered in a public trial registry or announced as started after the launch day; a phase 3 registered before the launch day does not count, nor do plans to start. Void when the trial's primary result came out before the close. Resolved after 31 December 2027 by two labellers (`next_step_label_instructions.md`). Shown, never ranked.

Optional, for any trial:

4. **`variability`**: for a continuous outcome, the standard deviation between patients, in the card's unit; for a time-to-event outcome, the number of primary events at the analysis. It is used only for the consistency check below.
5. **`main_risk`**: the most likely reason the trial would fail. It is one of "drug too weak", "control arm too strong", "wrong population", "endpoint or timing", "too small or underpowered", "safety or dropouts" or "other". With it, **`reason`**: one sentence on that risk, at most 400 characters. Both are published with the entry and never scored.
6. **`rationale`** and **`evidence`**: why you forecast what you did, in up to 150 words (and 1,500 characters), and up to 5 sources you relied on (NCT numbers, PMIDs, DOIs, links or a trial's name and year, each up to 300 characters). **On the Models board a rationale is required for every trial with `p_met`**: the round's instructions ask for it, in up to 100 words to keep runs cheap; the checker allows 150 for everyone. On the Open board it is optional, since an entry may be a statistical model with nothing to say in words. Control, direction-override and zero-width characters are refused. Never scored; published with the entry after the close, as plain text, so readers can see what the forecasts rest on. The organiser may withhold abusive text.
   **`assumptions`** (optional): up to 3 facts the forecast rests on that anyone can check, each as `{"claim": …, "source": …}` (each up to 300 characters): for example a control arm's result in an earlier trial and that trial's name and year. After the close the organiser's agents check up to 50 claims per entry, drawn with a random seed posted at the close, against their sources, as supported, not supported or not checkable. The share supported is published with the entry, with the counts and a 95% interval; an entry that gave none is shown as "none given". Never scored.

With the operations questions, for every trial with a `p_met` question (548 trials):

7. **`p_public`**: the probability that the trial gets a `p_met` score: that a public statement on its primary result comes out by the deadline (30 June 2027), in a press release, filing, congress abstract, paper or posted registry results. It counts whether the statement says the primary objective was met or not, or only calls the result positive or encouraging, which counts as not met at the deadline (rule 6); a stop with no public reason counts too (rule 5). It is decided by the labellers from the first pass's labels; it is void when the statement comes out before the close. With `p_met` it gives the chance of a met result by the deadline: `p_met` × `p_public`. **`p_public_mar`** and **`p_public_dec`**: the same by 31 March 2027 and by 31 December 2027, from the early and final passes' labels; so `p_public_mar` ≤ `p_public` ≤ `p_public_dec`, which the checker enforces.

Operations questions, for every trial (653 trials), answered from the trial's ClinicalTrials.gov record as it stands on 31 August 2027:

8. **`p_late`** (late per the registry): the probability that primary completion comes more than 90 days after the date registered on the launch date, as the registry shows it. It counts as late when, on 31 August 2027, the record gives a primary completion date more than 90 days after that date, or the trial is still recruiting (recruiting, not yet recruiting, or enrolling by invitation) more than 90 days after it. It is void when the trial has stopped, and void when the record's primary completion date is then still marked "estimated" although more than 90 days past (the sponsor has not said whether the trial finished). Asked only when the registered date is after the launch date (484 trials for a launch on 1 November), because a date that has passed is no longer a forecast.
9. **`p_stopped`**: the probability that the trial's status is terminated, suspended or withdrawn.
10. **`p_posted`**: the probability that results are posted on ClinicalTrials.gov. Most trials of the round are not yet legally due to post by the read date (US law asks for results about a year after primary completion), so most answers are expected to be low.
11. **`p_amended`**: the probability that between the launch day and 31 August 2027 the record's primary outcomes change in substance. Only these count: a primary outcome measure added or removed (splitting or merging entries without changing the measures does not count); the measured variable or its scale changed; a fixed assessment time changed (week 24 to week 52, say); or the comparison changed (superiority to non-inferiority). A change in the maximum follow-up of a time-to-event outcome, rewording, added detail and changes to secondary outcomes do not count. Judged by two labellers who compare the record as pulled for the round (its primary outcomes are in the packet, fingerprinted in the manifest) with the record fetched on 31 August 2027 by the same means (`amend_label_instructions.md`; disagreements settled by the organiser); void only when that fetch fails.

The packet ends with the trial's operations questions, its launch-day status and the exact day after which it counts as late.

The answers come in blocks, each all or none on its own:

- An entry that gives number answers gives `control` and `effect` for every number question. An entry without them is scored on `p_met` alone.
- An entry that gives subgroup answers gives them for every trial with subgroups, every subgroup and group the card lists.
- The same for `safety`, `p_key_secondary` and `p_next_step`: each for every trial that asks it, or for none.
- An entry that gives operations answers (`p_late`, `p_stopped`, `p_posted`) gives them for every trial, including the 105 with no `p_met` question. An entry without them sends no line for those 105 trials, unless it answers `p_amended`.
- `p_public`, `p_public_mar`, `p_public_dec` and `p_amended` are blocks of their own: each for every trial that asks it or for none, with or without the others.
- `p_met_mar`, `p_met_dec`, `rationale` (except on the Models board), `evidence` and `assumptions` may be given trial by trial.

All answers are JSON numbers: `0.5`, not `"0.5"` or `true`.

Cards get `"numbers": "no"` in five cases:

- the outcome's unit is arbitrary or specific to a device or lab, so no forecaster could know its scale;
- the trial has no control arm to compare with;
- the main result is not one number (a win ratio, a hierarchical composite);
- the trial is an equivalence trial, where the question is whether two products match;
- the trial has no efficacy goal to meet (card design `none`).

## Who can enter, and with what

Anyone. No primary result of these trials was found public at launch, so there is nothing to look up, and every entry goes on one of two boards:

- **Models board** (`setting`: `closed`): one model, the round's instructions (`forecast_instructions.md`) unchanged, the packet and card only, one answer per trial: no web, no tools that bring in information, no other model, no prompt of your own. The trials may be sent one at a time or in batches, and writing the answers to a file is fine. Give the exact model version as the provider names it, with its date or number (an alias such as "latest" is refused), and the provider or API; the page fills in the instructions' SHA-256 when you confirm you used them unchanged. Run settings (reasoning effort, temperature, dates) are welcome. If the model's answer for a trial fails the checker, that trial may be run again with the same model, instructions and settings, at most twice more, alone or with the batch it was sent in (whose answers are then all replaced by the new run's); the number of re-runs is declared in `run_settings` and shown with the entry. Every trial must be answered by the declared model: a trial the model refuses, or that the provider answers with another model, is run again under the same rule, and an entry still missing a trial after that is incomplete and refused. An entrant may also leave out a whole optional block. No other change to the model's output is allowed. The organiser's own runs follow the same rule and publish their re-run counts. The board rests on what entrants declare: the organiser cannot check how an entry was made, except for its own runs, whose logs it keeps and checks for any web search or fetch. The organiser may re-run 40 trials of any Models board entry placed clearly better, with the declared model and the round's instructions, and publish how closely they agree. The 40 trials are drawn with a random seed posted at the close. The entry counts as reproduced when the mean absolute difference in `p_met` over the 40 is at most 0.08. The threshold comes from the organiser's own runs on the round's prompt: 56 trials run twice by each of three models before the round opened. Two runs of the same model differed by 0.045 (Opus 5.5), 0.053 (Sonnet 5.5) and 0.062 (Haiku 5.5) on average; a 40-trial sample of the noisiest of the three differs by more than 0.08 about once in 100 draws, the other two almost never. Two different models can also pass: Sonnet 5.5 passed as Opus 5.5 in about half of the draws, Haiku 5.5 as Opus 5.5 in about 6%. So the check is of honesty, not identity: it catches a declared model far from the one used, not every substitution. Entry names, team names and model versions are short plain text (at most 80 characters, no control characters) and are shown escaped, so they cannot carry links or images onto the page.
- **Open board** (`setting`: `open`): anything else. Any model, any number of models, any prompt, tools, retrieval and web access, and people in the loop. Say how the entry was made, shown with it on the leaderboard: `retrieval` (one of "none", "web search", "own document collection", "web search and own collection"), and true or false for `human_in_the_loop` (people edited or chose forecasts), `fine_tuned` (a model was trained or fine-tuned for this) and `ensemble` (answers of several models or runs combined), with the harness in a paragraph and, if you like, a link to the code. Registry and literature APIs count as web search. `retrieval` must agree with `web_access`: the checker refuses "none" with web access, and web search without it.
- Report the average cost per trial in US dollars: compute and API costs, not people's time (say in the harness if people worked on the forecasts). There is no cap; it is shown with the entry as declared, not checked.
- Each Hugging Face account may hold up to three entries, each a different harness. Every entry is listed with the account that uploaded it. If an account ends up with more (for example from uploads at the same moment), its first three entries by first upload count, and the others are listed as left out right after the close.
- Say in the harness description if you work for, or advise, a sponsor of a trial in the round.

## Dates

- **Opens:** 1 November 2026.
- **Submissions close:** at the end of 15 November 2026, UTC: an upload received at 23:59:59 UTC counts, one at 00:00 UTC on 16 November does not. The window is kept short on purpose: a trial whose result comes out before the close is dropped, and many trials in the round complete their primary outcome in November and December.
- **Early pass:** 31 March 2027. The trials whose first public result comes after the close and by that date, scored on `p_met_mar` (or `p_met` where an entry gave none) and `p_public_mar`, against B0 and B1 worked out for that date. Its tiers are shown once, when its labels are complete and 100 trials are scored there, as an early look; it does not change the ranking. Fewer trials, and the quickest reporters.
- **Deadline for results:** 30 June 2027. The ranked result is this first pass: the trials whose first public result comes after the close and on or before the deadline. `p_met` is asked for exactly this set.
- **Final pass:** 31 December 2027. The same scores again, counting results up to that date, on `p_met_dec` (or `p_met`) and `p_public_dec`, shown beside the ranked result and never ranked. It covers more trials, including slower reporters, so it shows how far the June result holds; it does not change the ranking. It shows no skill against B0 or B1, whose June forecasts are not matched to its windows (see Reference entries). `p_next_step` is resolved after this date.
- Trials that report before the close are dropped and listed. This includes a trial found, when it is labelled, to have had a public result before the round opened that the checks before launch missed: its first public date comes before the close, so it is never scored.
- Trials still silent at the deadline of a pass are dropped from that pass. Their number is shown, with the share met among the trials scored next to the base rate, so anyone can see how far the reported trials lean towards success.
- The leaderboard is updated as results come out, at least once a month, with scores and intervals. Tiers are shown only once a pass's labels are complete: for the first pass after 30 June 2027 (the ranking), and for the early pass once, after 31 March 2027 (an early look). Looking at interim tiers every month would multiply the chance of a false "clearly better", so the monthly updates show none.
- **`p_public`:** scored once the labels are complete for each pass, after its deadline.
- **Operations questions:** read from the registry on 31 August 2027, two months after the deadline, so that sponsors have had time to update their records; scored then, `p_amended` included.

## Scoring

- **Headline: the Brier score of `p_met` in the first pass, against the registry model.** The Brier score is the mean squared difference between the forecast and the outcome (1 = met, 0 = not met); lower is better. Like any proper score it rewards calibration as well as sorting: forecasting the right share of successes counts, and since `p_met` is asked given a result by the deadline, knowing that trials which report lean towards success is part of the forecast.
- **Tiers.** Once the first pass's labels are complete and 100 trials are scored, every entry is placed in one of three tiers against the registry model (`ref-b1`): **clearly better** (a Brier score lower by more than 0.005), **no clear difference**, or **clearly worse** (higher by more than 0.005). Each entry is tested on its own, so no other entry, and no copy of the registry model, can move its tier. Both one-sided tests are fixed in advance for every entry. Their p-values come from a normal approximation with the standard deviation of the paired bootstrap below; the smaller one is doubled, so no direction is picked after seeing the data, and compared with 5%. An entry whose true gap is exactly at the margin is placed clearly better or worse about 2.5% of the time (1.7% to 3.4% in the simulation below), so in a large field a few tiers can be chance; the number of entries tested is shown with each board. With two ranked tracks and up to three entries per account, an account whose entries all sit exactly at the margin on both tracks would see at least one false "clearly better" about 14% of the time (1 − 0.975⁶); entries truly level with the reference are called far less often. "No clear difference" means the data cannot tell the entry from the registry model, not that the two are equal. Each board lists its entries by tier, then by Brier score. The tier is the ranking: the order within a tier, and comparisons between entries (shown as exploratory, with the same test, Holm-corrected over all pairs), are not. The organiser's model runs are on the Models board like any other entry.

  How large a lead "clearly better" takes, from a simulation with the round's sister groups (`sim_tiers.py`, seed 7, 1,000 simulated rounds per size; its output is published). The entry's errors are about as independent of the registry model's as in the pilots:

  | Trials scored | Brier score lower than the registry model's by about, to be placed clearly better half the time | 80% of the time | False "clearly better" at the margin (nominal 2.5%) | False "clearly worse" at the margin (nominal 2.5%) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 100 | 0.044 | 0.061 | 2.6% | 3.1% |
  | 200 | 0.033 | 0.045 | 1.7% | 3.0% |
  | 400 | 0.025 | 0.033 | 1.9% | 2.2% |
  | 548 (every trial with `p_met`) | 0.022 | 0.029 | 2.7% | 2.8% |

  (Simulation error: each rate has a standard error of about 0.5 points, so two runs can differ by 1.5 points or more; across this week's rebuilds the same cell has moved from 3.7% to 2.0%. Read the rates as "about 2–3%", not to the decimal.) The error sizes are set, not fitted: the registry model's forecasts err with a standard deviation of 0.9 on the logit scale, half of every error shared within a sister group. With smaller errors, more of them shared (0.6, and 80%), the lead needed half the time is about a third lower (0.030, 0.023, 0.018 and 0.016), with false calls of 1.4% to 3.2%; both runs are published. An entry built on the registry model's own forecasts, changing them only a little, varies far less from it and needs far less: about 0.013, 0.011, 0.009 and 0.009 at the same sizes, half the time. With a registry-model Brier score near 0.16 to 0.22, the first column is a skill of roughly 10% to 30% over it. In a field of 40 entries all exactly at the margin, about one would be placed clearly better by chance; entries truly level with the registry model are placed there far less often. Every pilot model was well short of this, so most entries are likely to land in "no clear difference". Shown with every entry:
  - a 95% bootstrap interval that resamples whole sister-trial groups (`sisters.json`: trials of the same experimental drug, whoever the sponsor), because such trials tend to succeed or fail together;
  - the skill against the base rate (`ref-b0`) and against the registry model (`ref-b1`), each with a paired interval from the same bootstrap. Both baselines' forecasts are published from the opening day, with their training table and script: they are public bars to beat, and copying one earns no more than its score. Both baselines are matched to each trial's own reporting window (see Reference entries), so they already carry the success rate of trials that report in time: beating the registry model takes knowing which trials will work, not only how many. The skill against the registry model is also shown with sponsor-level clusters (a sensitivity check) and separately, with intervals, for China-only trials and the rest, because the registry model leans on that feature;
  - **adds to the registry model** (exploratory): whether the entry carries information the registry model lacks, even if it loses to it on level. A logistic regression of the outcome on logit(`ref-b1`) and logit(entry), forecasts kept within 0.01–0.99, is fitted out of fold in 10 folds of whole sister groups (a small ridge, 0.01, on the two weights), and compared with the same regression on logit(`ref-b1`) alone, so recalibrating the registry model gains nothing by itself. The Brier gain is averaged over 5 random fold splits. Its uncertainty comes from 500 resamples of whole sister groups, each running the same estimator (the same 5 splits, every fold refitted); the 95% interval is the gain plus or minus 1.96 of their standard deviations. "Yes" needs the interval above 0 and a positive weight on the entry: an entry that helps only when turned upside down does not add, and its gain is not shown. Not corrected across entries; the number of entries tested is shown. Never ranked;
  - AUROC (how well the forecasts sort met from not met) and the Brier score's split into reliability (calibration), resolution (sorting) and the level gap (mean forecast minus the share met);
  - for an entry uploaded more than once, its first upload's Brier score and the paired difference, last minus first, with both uploads' setups (models, version, harness, settings). It is an upload-to-upload change: it measures the value of the extra weeks only when the setup stayed the same, and the leaderboard marks it when it did not.
- **The base rate and the registry model** are shown as baselines, on no board.
- **Also shown for `p_met`:** log loss and the calibration slope.
- **Early pass:** the same tiers, worked out the same way on the early pass's trials against B1 for 31 March 2027, are shown once, when the early pass's labels are complete and 100 trials are scored there, as an early look. They are not the ranking: few trials, and the quickest reporters. An entry that gave no `p_met_mar` for a trial is scored on its `p_met` there, and the share of trials where that happened is shown with the entry. The early pass has no number tiers.
- **Number score: the second ranking.** In the first pass, once its labels are complete and 100 number answers are scored (a control or an effect value counts as one), every entry that gave number answers is placed in a number tier against the frozen reference entry `ref-opus-5-5`: **clearly better** (a number score lower by more than 0.05), **no clear difference** or **clearly worse** (higher by more than 0.05), with the same test as the `p_met` tiers, on the paired difference over the resolved number questions, resampling whole sister groups. The reference entry itself gets no number tier. The number tier is shown in its own column; each board stays ordered by the `p_met` tier.

  How large a lead this takes, from a resampling of Pilot 3's 116 published numbers (64 effects and 52 control arms), scaled the same way, with trials kept whole, for three pilot model runs against Opus: to be placed clearly better half the time, a number score lower than the reference's by about 0.36 to 0.61 at 100 scored answers, 0.27 to 0.44 at 200 and 0.21 to 0.33 at 400 (80% of the time: 0.49 to 0.85, 0.37 to 0.60 and 0.27 to 0.45). An entry exactly at the margin was placed clearly better 0.8% to 3.5% of the time (nominal 2.5%), so the test holds up despite a few very large misses. `ref-opus-5-5` both sets the scale and is the comparator; in Pilot 3, a copy of it with its ranges widened gained less than the margin (at most 0.04), and the order of the models was the same whichever model's widths set the scale. The scripts and their output are published. The number score:
  - `low80`, `median` and `high80` are read as your 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles, and all three are scored. Each resolved answer gets the range's width, plus 10 times any miss, plus 5 times the distance from your median to the result. This is the percentile (pinball) loss, the same as the weighted interval score used by the COVID-19 forecast hubs (Bracher et al. 2021) up to a constant. Ratios and times are scored on the log scale.
  - To compare trials with different units, each score is divided by the width of the reference entry's range for the same question. The reference is `ref-opus-5-5`, frozen and fingerprinted before the round opens. No width counts as narrower than a floor, so a near-zero reference range cannot blow up one question: for time-to-event and responder questions, the 10th percentile of the reference's widths for that part (control or effect) and endpoint kind; for continuous questions, whose units differ, the 10th percentile of the reference's widths relative to each question's own size (the reference's control median plus its control range), times that question's size.
  - The number score is the plain mean over the resolved number questions, the average of a proper score, so honest ranges do best. The median and the mean with the top and bottom 5% left out are shown beside it, to show how much a few large misses move it; they are not used to compare entries, because trimming would reward ranges narrower than the forecaster believes. Lower is better; a range as wide as the reference's, with the median on the result, scores 1.
  - The share of results that fell inside the 80% ranges is shown too; well-calibrated ranges hold about 80%.
- **Subgroup score:** shown next to the ranking, not part of it. Each resolved subgroup effect is scored like an effect answer, divided by the width of the reference entry's range for the same group (never less than the effect floor). Sex and the subgroups the record gives make the subgroup score; the commonly reported ones are shown apart, as experimental; sex is also shown on its own.
- **Safety score:** shown next to the ranking, not part of it. Each resolved share is scored with the same percentile loss, in percentage points (every trial shares the unit, so nothing is divided), with the share inside the 80% ranges.
- **Key secondary and next step:** the Brier score of `p_key_secondary` (on the trials whose key secondary result is public by the pass's deadline) and of `p_next_step` (after 31 December 2027; void for trials that reported before the close), each against the share in the round itself. Shown next to the ranking, not part of it.
- **Assumptions:** the share of an entry's checked assumptions that its sources support, with the counts. Shown, never scored.
- **Consistency:** shown, not scored. It measures how far the stated `p_met` is from the `p_met` implied by the entry's own `control`, `effect` and `variability`, worked through the card's arm sizes and significance level.
- **`p_public` score:** the Brier score of `p_public` on the trials that did not report before the close, against the share public by the deadline in the round itself; in the early and final passes, of `p_public_mar` and `p_public_dec`. Shown next to the ranking, not part of it.
- **Met by the deadline:** the Brier score of `p_met` × `p_public` against "a statement on the primary result after the close and by the deadline, and met", on the same trials, against the share in the round itself, for entries that gave `p_public` for every trial. It answers a different question from the headline (will this trial deliver a met readout in time?) and is shown next to the ranking, not part of it.
- **Operations score:** shown next to the ranking, not part of it, so that knowing how sponsors run their trials cannot lift an entry in a ranking about medicine.
  - The Brier score of each operations question, and of `p_late`, `p_stopped` and `p_posted` pooled; `p_amended` is shown on its own and is not in the pool.
  - The reference is the share in the round itself (for example, the share of trials that ended up late), worked out after the read date. There is no history of registry records to fit a base rate on in advance, so this reference is computed after the fact and says so.
  - Skill is 1 minus the entry's Brier score divided by the reference's.

## Labels

A trial's label is set by its first public disclosure of whether the primary objective was met:

- a press release;
- a regulatory or stock-exchange filing;
- a congress abstract or presentation;
- a paper;
- results posted in a public registry.

Labels are made from public sources by the organiser's labelling agents, and every label is checked by a second, independent labeller that does not see any forecast. Disagreements are settled by the organiser, and the agreement rate is published. Labels are published with their sources at each update and corrected in the open; the models used for labelling are named with them.

The rules for "met":

1. The primary objective as the trial defined it:
   - superiority at the trial's own level, or non-inferiority against its own pre-specified margin; when the trial goes on to test superiority, non-inferiority is enough;
   - the trial's own hypothesis decides, also when the card inferred a different design;
   - only the card's primary outcome at its pre-specified level within the trial's own testing order counts;
   - a numerical improvement without significance, or a "clinically meaningful" one without significance, is not met;
   - a win only in a subgroup, a per-protocol set or a post-hoc analysis is not met.
2. In superiority and non-inferiority trials, only efficacy counts. Safety, blood-level and lot-consistency outcomes listed as primary do not. (Equivalence trials: rule 9.)
3. Several efficacy primary outcomes:
   - in trials with parts, only the randomized confirmatory part counts;
   - co-primary outcomes must all be met by the same arm, unless they split the significance level, in which case either one counts;
   - otherwise the sponsor's own statement of whether the primary endpoint was met decides.
4. Several doses or experimental arms: one arm meeting the objective against the registered primary comparison is enough.
5. Interim analyses and early stops:
   - an interim analysis that declares the primary objective met is met; one that neither declares it met nor stops the trial leaves the question open;
   - stopped for futility or harm is not met;
   - stopped with a public reason that is not about efficacy (business, slow enrolment, a supply problem) and no efficacy result is dropped and listed;
   - stopped after an unblinded interim analysis with no public reason, or "after reviewing the data", is not met.
6. "Positive" or "encouraging" with no statement on the primary endpoint keeps the question open. If nothing clearer comes by the deadline of the pass, it is not met in that pass. This is the round's choice, on the view that a sponsor which talks about its trial without claiming the primary endpoint has more often missed it; it is an assumption, not something measured in this cohort, so these trials are marked and every score is also shown without them. (A trial vague in June and clear by December can therefore differ between the two passes; the ranked pass uses June.)
7. Labels are judged against the primary outcomes in the round's packet. If the sponsor reports a different endpoint as primary and never reports the registered one, it is not met.
8. Sister trials are labelled one by one. A pooled-only result counts as vague (rule 6).
9. Equivalence trials: met when the sponsor reports that equivalence (bioequivalence, biosimilarity, comparability or lot consistency) was shown on the primary outcomes, within the protocol's margins (no record in the round states them). The blood-level, efficacy and immune-response comparisons listed as primary all count; safety outcomes do not. When PK and efficacy are both primary, both must succeed.

**`p_public` labels.** From the first pass's labelling, after the deadline: 1 when the first public statement on the primary result (a vague one or a stop with no public reason included) came after the close and by 30 June 2027; 0 when none was public by then; void when it came before the close, or the trial left the round (a stop for a reason not about efficacy, rule 5) by the deadline; a trial that leaves the round after a pass's deadline counts as 0 in that pass. The same for `p_public_mar` and `p_public_dec`, each from its own pass's labels; the met-by-the-deadline score of each pass uses that pass's two fields. A pass is re-scored when its labels are corrected, and the change is listed.

**Dates.** Labellers record two dates: the first public statement on the primary result, vague ones included, and the first clear one (whether the objective was met). The first decides whether a trial reported before the close (dropped) or after a pass's deadline (not scored in it). The second decides rule 6: a trial that was only vague at a pass's deadline counts as not met in that pass, even if it became clear later, and is marked as such; so is a pooled-only result (rule 8). A stop with no public reason is not met (rule 5) but is not marked: it is a stop, not a vague statement. A met, not-met or changed-endpoint label needs the clear date. Numbers count in a pass only if published by its deadline. When the two labellers' dates fall on different sides of the close or of a deadline, or a date is malformed, the trial goes to the organiser, so that one labeller's date alone never decides whether a trial is dropped, scored or late.

**Operations labels.** Read automatically from each trial's record, fetched from the ClinicalTrials.gov API on 31 August 2027, against the launch-day row in `operations.jsonl` (`tdcb trials round ops-resolve`). No judgement is involved, except for `p_amended`: two labellers compare the primary outcomes of the record as pulled for the round with the record fetched on 31 August 2027, against the closed list of changes above (`amend_label_instructions.md`); disagreements go to the organiser.

**Key secondary labels.** By the deadline of the pass, under the rule given with the question: met when a source reports it significant in favour of the card's experimental arm and does not say it was outside formal testing; not met for any other reported result (not significant, not formally tested, or reported without a test); void when no result is public, when it is reported only as immature with a later formal analysis planned, or when the trial left the round.

**Safety labels.** For each of the card's arms, by the deadline of the pass, from a press release, congress or paper (not the registry's posted results): the first published share stopping any study treatment for an adverse event (the safety population); if none, the share stopping the experimental drug or its placebo; if none, the share leaving the study for an adverse event; for an arm with nothing to stop, the share leaving the study for an adverse event. Each arm resolves on its own; void when none of these is published.

**Next-step labels.** After 31 December 2027, by two labellers searching public sources and trial registries (`next_step_label_instructions.md`): 1 when the card's next step, as defined with the question, was announced after the launch day and by 31 December 2027, else 0; a statement linking an application to the trial counts only if it too is public by 31 December 2027. Void for trials that reported before the close or left the round by 31 December 2027. Scored once, with the last pass's labels (December 2027).

**Number labels.**

- A number question resolves with the first public disclosure, by the deadline of the pass, that gives the number for the card's endpoint, time point, arms and comparison.
- A number for another time point, dose or population does not count, and the question stays void unless the right one comes out.
- A least-squares or adjusted mean difference counts as the difference in means. For a responder card, a result given only as an odds or risk ratio is converted to a difference in percentage points when both arms' rates are published; otherwise the effect question is void.
- When the effect is published without the control arm's value, only the control answer is void.
- Unpublished numbers make the question void; the `p_met` question still counts.
- A subgroup question resolves with the first public effect estimate within each group, as the card defines the groups, for the card's endpoint and comparison (a forest plot counts), by the deadline of the pass. A group resolves only when the publication reports exactly that group; a group the publication cuts differently (70 years instead of 65, or three bands instead of two) is void. Each group resolves on its own.

## Fair play

No inside information about a trial's result or its timelines, and no use of other entries' forecasts. The organiser may ask the top entries for their code or logs and may remove an entry that breaks these rules. A team holds at most three entries in all, whatever the number of accounts it uploads from; the organiser removes the extra ones, keeping the team's three earliest. After the close, the organiser also looks for entries that are confident and right far more often on one sponsor's trials than on the rest.

## Reference entries

The organiser registers these before the round opens and posts their SHA-256s, with the manifest's, outside the submission page. The base rate and the registry model are published in full on the opening day; the model entries stay private until the close:

- the base rate (B0) and a model on seven registry fields (B1), `p_met` and `p_met_mar` only, fitted on earlier company trials. Both are published with their training table, the label audit and the script (`make_baselines.py`), so anyone can refit them byte for byte.
  - **Training trials.** 218 earlier company trials, all completed in 2026, whose primary result was public before the round opened and came at a time after primary completion that the round can score (between 3.7 months before and 12 months after; 93 of them are Pilot 2's trials). Stops with no result are left out, since the check did not record their reason; the round counts a stop with no public reason as not met, so B1 may run slightly high on trials likely to stop.
  - **Labels, read as the round reads them.** Each training trial keeps the date of its first public statement and the date its result became clear. For a window ending b months after primary completion, a training trial counts as met only if its result was clearly met by then; a result still vague, reported on another endpoint than the registered one, or clear only later counts as not met (rules 6 and 7). A training trial counts for a window only if its first result came within it and it was followed for all of it: its primary completion plus b is on or before the check date, 25 October 2026. The others are left out, whatever their label: early results lean met, so keeping the clear ones and leaving out only the vague ones would push the rate up.
  - **Checks of the labels.** The 54 training labels whose first result came more than a month before completion were checked by web search: all but one were the trial's own readout (often an interim win, with the completion date moved later), 13 got an earlier first-disclosure date, 3 are safety stops (not met, rule 5), and 1 could not be confirmed and is left out; the trials that reported years early are outside the scored lags and not used. Since the audit moved 13 of 54 dates earlier (a press release before the paper), some other training dates may also be late; this mainly blurs the window matching. All 23 labels first called vague or changed endpoint were checked again by two independent labellers on 28 October, without seeing the first call. They agreed on 18 of the 23: 7 clear results public before the check date (6 met, 1 not met; corrected), 9 still vague, and 2 where neither found anything. The organiser decided the other 7 (the 5 disagreements, and the 2 where neither labeller found anything but the first check had found a vague statement) from the sources the labellers and the first check cited, opened again: 1 met (a sponsor's statement that only one labeller had found), 4 vague, and 2 left out (an interim report with no primary result, and a stop). Both re-checks and every decision, with its reasons and source, are published (`baselines_relabel_check_a.jsonl`, `_b.jsonl` and `baselines_label_audit.jsonl`).
  - **B0.** Each round trial is scored only if its result comes after the close and by 30 June 2027, a window that depends on its primary completion date. B0 is the share met at the window's end among the training trials that count for that same window after their own primary completion, shrunk towards 63.8% (the share met in B1's fit) with a weight of 20 trials. It runs from 0.58 to 0.72. The round trials that completed earliest have the longest windows, and fewer training trials were followed that long: for the 65 that completed by early September 2026 (windows ending 10–12 months out) there are none, so their B0 is 63.8%; 109 round trials have fewer than 20 behind them, and the median round trial has 105.
  - **B1.** B1 moves B0, on the logit scale, by a logistic regression on phase 3, placebo control, enrolment, cancer, large company, China only and time-to-event endpoint (C = 0.1), and is kept within 0.05–0.95. The regression is fitted once, on the median round trial's window (from 0.2 months before to 6.5 months after primary completion): 105 training trials, 63.8% met. One fit for every window is an approximation. Fitted on the window of the round trial at the 25th or the 75th percentile instead, B1's mean over the round would be 0.60 or 0.67 (it is 0.63); with the 7 organiser decisions left out of training, 0.66 (`baselines_info.json`, `sensitivity`).
  - **The early and final passes.** For the early pass, each round trial's window ends on 31 March 2027 instead, and B0 and B1 are worked out for it the same way, with the same fit (B1's regression is not refitted); they are `p_met_mar`. Every round trial has training trials followed for its whole early window. B0 runs from 0.55 to 0.69 for 31 March (mean 0.63, against 0.64 for 30 June), and B1's mean is 0.61 (0.63 for 30 June). For the final pass, no training trial was followed long enough (windows ending up to 18 months out), so B0 and B1 have no `p_met_dec`; the final pass shows them with their June forecasts but no entry's skill against them.
  - **China-only trials.** None of the 40 China-only training trials was a clear failure: 34 were met and 6 were only vague. In B1's fit, 13 were met and 2 count as not met, both only vague. B1 leans on that feature (0.76 on China-only trials, 0.58 on the rest; 0.85 on China-only trials if the 7 organiser decisions are left out), and its China-only effect may measure how often such sponsors report vaguely as much as how often they succeed. Hence the split shown next to its skill.
- Models board runs by Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, under the Models board's rules (the round's instructions unchanged, the packet and card only, no web), with every question, the operations questions included. They are placed in tiers like any Models board entry (`ref-opus-5-5` gets no number tier, being the number score's reference). A batch with an answer the checker refused, or whose log showed a tool other than reading the instructions and the batch and writing the answers, was run again whole, at most twice, as the Models board allows; the re-run counts and reasons are in their metadata (a first log check also refused runs that only tidied their own answer file, since allowed). One trial, NCT07385287 (a live attenuated malaria-parasite vaccine), was taken out of the round before it opened: a safety filter stopped Opus 5.5 on it in every run and the provider answered with another model, so no complete Opus entry could include it. Taking the trial out, rather than the Opus entry, is a selection: the round no longer holds the one trial the organiser's reference model would not answer. An outside entry whose model refuses a trial of the round cannot be completed and is refused; tell the organiser before the close if that happens, and it will be listed. Their exact model versions, run settings, run dates and the instructions' SHA-256 are in their metadata.

`ref-opus-5-5` is also the reference that scales the number score.

The model runs are closed book, so running them later would change nothing: they see the same packet with the same model. Entries with web access can use news that comes out while the round is open; the leaderboard therefore shows every entry's board, web access and last upload date next to its score, and scores first uploads too.
