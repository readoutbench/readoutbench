# Round 1, rebuilt: how the round is made

The rules are in the round's rules (the rules in the repository; `1_design/01_round1_rules_draft.md` in the review package). The steps:

1. **Trial list** (`build_list.py`).
   - Who: company trials from the Round 2 candidates and Round 1, primary completion July 2026 to March 2027.
   - Left out, with the NCT numbers of every step in `funnel.json`:
     - stale-status trials (still recruiting after their own end date);
     - stopped trials;
     - trials already reported;
     - one record that calls itself a mock study;
     - sponsors that are individuals (a name with M.D., Ph.D. or IRB) or non-profits, whatever the registry's sponsor class says (`INDIVIDUAL`, `NOT_A_COMPANY`);
     - sponsors not expected to give a verifiable result (`SUSPECT_SPONSORS`: six single-site trials of unapproved peptides registered in two weeks, one a copy of Lilly's SURMOUNT-1).
   - A single-site trial with 1,000 or more patients stops the build until it is looked at by hand (`BIG_SINGLE_SITE_OK` holds the three looked at and kept).
   - Each trial gets its questions:
     - "main": `p_met` and, where the card says so, the number questions;
     - "equivalence": `p_met`, where met means equivalence shown (13 trials: biosimilars, bioequivalence, lot consistency);
     - "operations only": trials with no efficacy goal to meet (162: safety or drug levels only, descriptive, no control group, doses compared with no control, and `OPS_ONLY`).
   - Every trial also gets the operations questions (`p_late`, `p_stopped`, `p_posted`); every trial with `p_met` also gets `p_public` (added by the export).
   - The 21 trials the reader marked "review" go to `review.json` and stay out.
   - `--today` marks trials past their primary completion that have no results check ("not checked"); a results check passed with `--labels` is recorded as "web check: nothing public", with its date when the label row has `checked_on`; `CHECKED_BY_HAND` holds the two checked by a web search on 26 October.
   - `DISCLOSED` marks (in `note`) the ten supplement, probiotic, device or contract-research trials, which stay in.
   - `STOP_ANNOUNCED`: four trials whose stop was announced in public before launch, found by a web check of the operations-only trials past their completion date (27 October); out.
   - Result on 27 October: 836 trials (665 main, 13 equivalence, 158 operations only). After the results check of every trial (step 2) and the records pulled again on 29 October: 717 (581 main, 13 equivalence, 123 operations only); after the final results check, the deeper check, the strict recheck and the stop check (step 2b): 653 (536 main, 12 equivalence, 105 operations only), 85 of them kept below the bar (below). The results check's drops are their own steps in `funnel.json` (result public, stop announced, unclear after the second check), from label rows with `reported` "yes", "stopped_without_result" or "unclear".
2. **Results check of every trial**, not only those past their primary completion date: a trial can report its primary result at an interim analysis years before the date it registered (EVER-132-002 and RUBY did). First PubMed by NCT number (`pilot/round2/plan_pubmed.py`, retrieval agents, `collect_pubmed.py`, which reads the results out of the agents' transcripts, then `make_pubmed_packets.py` and judge agents with `pubmed_judge_instructions.md`, then `make_hints.py`); then a web check of every trial (`plan_leak.py`, `leak_check_instructions.md`: results, interim readouts and announced stops), each row dated (`checked_on`). Every trial found reported, stopped or with a stop announced is checked again by a second agent before it is dropped; the trials checked while the web-search budget had run out are checked again. Earlier checks (25 and 26 October, 148 trials, as in Round 2) carry no per-trial date. Done on 29 October for all 836: 130 findings, each checked by a second agent (`plan_verify.py`, `leak_verify_instructions.md`); 121 trials out; the organiser's decisions on the findings the second check did not confirm are published with the labels.
2b. **Final results check** (29 October), independent of step 2: `final_check_instructions.md` (via `plan_leak.py --instructions`), run by agents that did not do step 2, counting a public statement on any registered primary outcome (efficacy, equivalence, PK, immunogenicity, lot consistency, or safety where safety is the primary) and searching for programme and pooled readouts; every row lists its `queries` and `pages_checked`. Then a **deeper check** (29-30 October, `final_check_deep_instructions.md`) of every trial whose first row did not meet the bar of `results_gate.py`: at least two distinct searches, one in Chinese, Japanese or Korean for a trial run only in East Asia, and one opened page that is not a registry listing, dated on or after the records' pull. Every trial with a finding in any row is out, with no case-by-case keep; `launch.py` refuses to build while a trial with a finding is not in its drop list, and leaves out any trial without a clean row. Result: 20 out for results or stops (8 from the final check, 7 from the deeper checks, 5 found by outside reviewers), 1 for the Opus fallback, 4 with an overdue topline (NCT05456425, NCT06989918, NCT06111235, NCT06776432), 2 with a public statement that covers or may cover a primary (NCT07669454, NCT06474806), 45 left out because no check reached the bar before launch (`unchecked_step` in the config, or listed in the drops after the native-language sweep), 640 in. Then, on 30 October: a stop announced before launch found by an outside reviewer (NCT04442022); a **strict recheck** (`strict_recheck_instructions.md`) of the 71 trials whose row carried a doubt the checking agent raised, requiring an opened page about the trial or its drug (1 result found, 7 kept with an organiser's sign-off because the doubt was only an imminent readout, 35 left out); and a **stop check** (`stop_check_instructions.md`) of the other 568 for stops, pauses and holds the registry does not show (6 out, 1 left out because it could not be searched); then the 11 trials whose only opened page was an exchange-filing copy on pdf.dfcfw.com, now refused, were rechecked (4 cleared, 7 left out). 589 in. Then the organiser put back the 88 trials left out because no check reached the bar: `below_bar` in the config lists them (`launch_inputs/below_bar.jsonl`), `launch.py` keeps them although their row is not clean (a finding still stops the build) and marks each `"check": "below the bar"` in `questions.json`; scores are shown with and without them. A third outside review took out 3 (safety statements), and a **safety-statement scan** (`safety_statement_check_instructions.md`) of the 179 trials whose primary is safety, PK or immune response, or that have no efficacy goal, took out 6 more; a fourth review took out NCT06292923 (topline imminent). For the trials below the bar the build waives every depth and date reason (no deep row, the agent's doubt flag, no page, language, searches, date); a finding in any row, or a safety statement on a safety primary (for any trial), still stops the build. A fifth review took out NCT04208178 (Part 1 DLTs at AACR 2023) and the two livmoniplimab trials AbbVie removed from its pipeline in February 2026; a sixth took out 5 more (a third livmoniplimab trial, three public statements on operations-only primaries, one sponsor statement on an efficacy primary), and a second look at the operations-only trials (`ops_recheck_instructions.md`, aimed at congress abstracts, advisory-committee slides, investor decks and local trade press, but in practice one or two web searches per trial) found nothing more. `results_gate.py` reads a safety statement before any other reason, an "unchecked" status included, so no other reason can hide one. A congress sweep (`congress_sweep_instructions.md`) then searched the 2026 abstract books (ESMO 2026 and the other autumn congresses) for every oncology trial and every trial past its primary completion, one search each. An eighth review then found LUMINET-1's primary result in an ESMO Asia 2025 late-breaking abstract (and RABI-767's sponsor statement on its safety primary); both are out, and a second sweep (`congress_sweep2_instructions.md`) searched 263 trials with two or three queries each, aimed at ESMO Asia 2025, CSCO, ASCO GI and ESMO GI, AAIC and CTAD, and Chinese-language sponsor news, and found nothing more. `results_gate.py` now reads a safety statement anywhere in a row (notes, quote, result and the snippets in `pages_checked`) and in every row of the trial, so a later clean row cannot hide an earlier one; a hit the organiser judges to be about another study is signed off (`signed_off.jsonl`: trial, reason and date, all required and checked; a trial not in the round stops the build). A sign-off covers the check rows dated on or before it: a newer row with a safety mention or a review flag needs a new sign-off. In Chinese, Japanese and Korean text the gate counts any mention of safety, tolerability or adverse events (安全, 耐受, 耐容, 忍容, 不良反应, 副反应, 反应原性, 有害事象, 副作用, 안전, 내약, 이상 반응, 유해사례…, matched with spaces taken out, and AE, SAE, TEAE or TRAE written inside such text), judgement or not: on a trial with a safety primary it needs the organiser's sign-off. A phrase list was tried and kept missing new phrasings. A ninth review found a trial its sponsor had discontinued while the registry still said active (TROPION-Lung12), so on 31 October every trial of the 37 large sponsors (195) was checked against the sponsor's pipeline documents and quarterly reports (`disc_sweep_instructions.md`, `quarterly_read_instructions.md`); that found AstraZeneca's MONO-OLA1 result in its third-quarter report. Both are out, with one stale record. `launch.py` writes `trial_below_bar` into `sisters.json`, and `round.leaderboard` shows every entry's Brier score, skill against the registry model and tier also without them (`without_below_bar`; "not_computable" when fewer than two scored trials are left); the ranking itself uses every scored trial. The config's `checks` now also read the stop check and the safety-statement scan (`other_checks.jsonl`, rows mapped to `reported`/`stop_announced`, listed first so that a finding there sticks while the deep row stays the one the bar reads). `results_gate.py` now also refuses a row whose notes record a safety statement ("well tolerated", "favourable safety profile" and the like) on a trial with such a primary, unless the organiser signs it off. 653 in.
3. **Question cards**.
   - Main trials: Opus agents read each record and write the card (`card_instructions.md`). `apply_fixes.py` applies `card_fixes.jsonl` (the reviews' corrections). Then other agents add the key secondary endpoint and, on phase 3 cards with number questions, up to two subgroups next to sex (`card_additions_instructions.md`, from the record alone); `make_cards.py --additions` merges them and sets the next step asked about from the phase.
   - Equivalence trials: `data/cards_equivalence.jsonl`, written by hand from the records.
   - Operations-only trials: made by `make_cards.py` (design `none`, with the reason the trial has no efficacy goal).
   - `make_cards.py` puts the three together, adds the subgroup question (phase 3, number questions, both sexes), for non-inferiority cards `design_basis` ("stated" when the record says non-inferiority, else "inferred": 19 of 27), and `measure_basis` "assumed" where the card's own notes say its writer had to assume the measure's unit, time point, scale or form (48 cards; choices about arms, doses or which primary comes first do not count). Packets say so, and say "margin not stated in the record".
4. **Card review** (`review_instructions.md`). Independent agents that did not write the cards check them against the records: 60 cards on 26 October (3 wrong, all from one rule, fixed) and 90 more after the outside reviews (all 27 non-inferiority cards, the cards with odd arm sizes or time-to-event wording, 20 multi-arm cards and a random rest): 0 wrong, 13 minor, 4 of them fixed. On 30–31 October every card, with its key secondary endpoint and subgroups, is checked by agents that wrote none of it; their fixes go into `card_fixes.jsonl` and the additions file.
5. **Sister groups** (`make_sisters.py`): trials of the same experimental drug, whoever the sponsor (interventions split into their drugs, codes mapped to names), for the clustered bootstrap: at the launch build, 548 `p_met` trials in 474 groups. The file also holds each trial's sponsor (for the sponsor-clustered sensitivity check) and whether it runs only in China (for the split of skill against B1).
6. **Staging** (`stage_round.py`, with `--snapshot` the day the records were pulled, `--sisters`, `--early-deadline 2027-03-31` (the early pass; with it every trial with `p_met` asks `p_public` for each pass and every trial asks `p_amended`), and `--instructions`, by default `forecast_instructions.md`: the Models board's prompt, copied into the round with its SHA-256 in the manifest).
   - Writes the records as a registry, labels every question open, writes each trial's operations row, builds the Track C set and exports the round with its cards, operations rows, sister groups, deadline and final deadline.
   - The manifest holds the SHA-256 of the packets, the cards, the operations rows and the sister groups.
   - Then `make_baselines.py` (which also writes `p_met_mar`, B0 and B1 for the early pass's windows, with the same fit) `--corrections data/baselines_label_audit.jsonl --pin data/baselines_relabel_check_a.jsonl data/baselines_relabel_check_b.jsonl` fits B0 and B1 on the 218 earlier company trials whose first result came at a lag the round can score, each with its first-public and clear dates. For each round trial's reporting window, a training trial counts only if its first result came within the same window after its own primary completion and the check came after the window's end, and it is read as the round would read it there: met only if clearly met by then; vague, on a changed endpoint, or clear only later is not met. B0 is that share, shrunk towards the share in B1's fit; B1's registry-field model is fitted once, on the median round trial's window (105 trials), and `baselines_info.json` gives its sensitivity to the window and to the organiser's label decisions. Both are registered as reference entries. The 23 labels first called vague or changed endpoint were checked again by two independent labellers (`data/baselines_relabel_check_a.jsonl`, `_b.jsonl`): they agreed on 18 (7 clear before the check date, corrected), and the organiser decided the other 7 from the cited sources; every decision is in the audit file. The audit file also records the web check of the 54 training labels with a first result more than a month before completion (50 the trial's own readout, 13 of them redated; 3 safety stops; 1 dropped). The script also writes the training table and the round trials' features, from which anyone can refit both byte for byte (`--train-table ... --round-features ...`, with the same `--corrections`, which it then checks against the table, and `--pin`). The baselines' metadata records the SHA-256 of the script and of all six files; the build refuses any other set.
7. **Launch, in this order, all before the round opens; the round's files are published once:**
   Steps 3 (from the selected list on), 4, the registration in 5, 6 and 7's build run as one command: `launch.py <config.json> [--dry-run]` (the config, documented in the script, names the list, the drops with their funnel step, the results-check label files and the reference entries). It stops at the first failure, refuses any trial left in whose latest check row is not a clean, deep enough "no" (`min_queries`, `min_opened`; with `unchecked_step` set, such trials go to the supplement instead), and builds the Space only against the registry SHA-256 it has just printed. Run it with `--dry-run` into a scratch folder first; the real run differs only in its out folder.
   1. pull every record again from the ClinicalTrials.gov API (`tdcb.trials.records.fetch`, from a machine that can reach it), so the records keep the ESTIMATED/ACTUAL type of the primary completion date;
   2. drop trials that stopped or posted results; run a full web results check on every trial past its primary completion date (not only the ones whose record changed: a press release does not change the record), and a web check of all 836 trials for a stop announced in public but not yet in the registry (as `STOP_ANNOUNCED`); pass the checks to `build_list.py --labels`, each row with `checked_on`;
   3. rerun `build_list.py --today <pull day>`, re-card the trials whose primary outcomes changed, rebuild the sister groups;
   4. stage once with `--snapshot <pull day> --opens <launch day>`, check every packet against the questions the checker requires (`check_packets.py <round dir>`, which must report 0 differences), and fit and register B0 and B1;
   5. run the model reference entries under the Models board's rules (`forecast_instructions.md` unchanged: Opus, Sonnet and Haiku, closed book, every question): `make_model_batches.py`, one agent per batch with the prompt it writes, then `check_model_runs.py` (refuses any transcript that used a tool other than Read, Write and Edit, read another file than the instructions and its batch, wrote or edited another file than its own output, left a batch incomplete, has a reply that does not name the declared model (another model or none), or leaves a trial of the round without a checked run; an agent that edits its own output file brings in nothing, so that is allowed). A trial the model will not answer after the re-runs makes the entry incomplete; for Round 1 the one such trial (NCT07385287, answered only by a fallback model) was taken out of the round. A trial whose answer the checker refuses is run again with its whole batch (the new run's answers replace the batch's), at most twice, as the rules allow; record the re-run counts in `run_settings`. Register them, each with `setting` closed, its exact `model_version`, `provider`, `run_settings` and the instructions' SHA-256 as `prompt_sha256` (the checker refuses them otherwise). Re-run 50 trials of each model once more and publish the run-to-run difference in `p_met` (the Models board's reproduction threshold is re-measured on this prompt). Done for Round 1 on 29 October: 7 batches drawn with seed 20261029 (56 trials with `p_met`), mean |difference| 0.045 (Opus), 0.053 (Sonnet), 0.062 (Haiku); the check became 40 trials at 0.08;
   6. `anchor.py <round dir>`: post the printed fingerprints (the manifest, the registry, the reference entries and the three labelling instructions) outside the submission page (a GitHub release or gist, a public post, an e-mail to a few outside people) before the round opens; while the round is open, post `anchor.py --registry-dir <copy of the public registry/>` every week;
   7. build the Space and the public dataset with `space/build_space.py --expect-registry-sha <the registry SHA-256 just posted>` (it refuses a registry that is not the posted one: a coordinated edit of an entry and the registry passes every other check), upload them, check the page with a test entry, then open.
8. **While the round is open:** nothing changes in the round's files. Entries may be uploaded again; each upload adds a public row.
9. **At the close:**
   - `space/pull_entries.py` once (it refuses to run earlier; it registers each entry's last upload, and its first upload as `<entry>.first` when it was uploaded more than once; it writes `left_out_entries.json` for any account over three entries, to publish with the entries), then `anchor.py <round dir> --registry-dir <copy of the public registry/>` and post the fingerprint again;
   - a full web check of every trial for results that came out before the close (dropped and listed).
10. **As results come out, and at each pass (31 March 2027: the early pass; 30 June 2027: the ranked result; 31 December 2027: supplementary):**
    - two labellers per trial, working independently and never seeing a forecast (`label_instructions_r1.md`, with the card, the four number fields and the Round 1 rules); the organiser adds results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov, read from its API, to each list entry;
    - `finalize_labels.py --deadline <the pass's deadline> --manifest <round dir>/manifest.json` turns the two readings into labels (a trial still vague at the deadline is not met in that pass, even if it became clear later; numbers count only if published by the deadline), lists the disagreements for the organiser, and writes the agreement rate (over trials where a result was found) and Cohen's kappa, which are published; run it once per pass;
    - `finalize_labels.py` also carries the secondary answers (safety shares, whether the key secondary endpoint was met, subgroup effects) where both labellers give them by the deadline and agree; the others, and any that are malformed, undated or not on the card, go to `secondary_disagreements.jsonl` for the organiser. A decision row carries only the fields it decides: with `reported` it decides the main label, and each secondary field it carries replaces that field alone. Every label row records the deadline and as-of day it was read for; the leaderboard refuses a pass whose key was built from another pass's labels;
    - build, and run `tdcb trials round leaderboard --pass first` (ranked: tiers against the registry model on each board, and number tiers against `ref-opus-5-5`), `--pass early` (tiers shown as an early look) or `--pass final` (supplementary, never ranked, and no skill against B0 or B1). The labels' as-of day comes from the key (or `--labels-as-of`). Tiers are shown only once a pass's labels are as of after its deadline; the monthly updates before that show scores and intervals only. With complete labels, the leaderboard also scores that pass's `p_public` and "met by the deadline" (`p_met` × `p_public`). The early pass refuses to run unless B0 and B1 carry `p_met_mar`.
11. **On 31 August 2027:**
    - fetch every trial's record again from the API (with the date type);
    - run `tdcb trials round ops-resolve --round-dir ... --records ... --out ops_outcomes.jsonl`;
    - pass `--ops-outcomes ops_outcomes.jsonl` to the leaderboard;
    - two labellers compare each record's primary outcomes as pulled for the round with the record fetched that day (`amend_label_instructions.md`, `p_amended`); pass their agreed outcomes (`{"nct_id", "amended": 1, 0 or null}`) as `amend_outcomes`.
12. **After 31 December 2027:** two labellers check each trial's next step (`next_step_label_instructions.md`; `p_next_step`, the card's `next_step`); pass the agreed outcomes (`{"nct_id", "next_step": 1, 0 or null}`) as `next_step_outcomes` (the leaderboard voids trials that reported before the close). After the close, the organiser's agents check up to 50 assumptions per entry, drawn with a seed posted at the close, against their sources; the counts per entry are passed as `assumption_checks`.

`template/` holds an example forecast file and metadata for both boards. `sim_tiers.py` is the simulation behind the rules' table of the lead "clearly better" needs (`data/sim_tiers.json`, seed 7; `data/sim_tiers_sensitivity.json` with `--registry-sd 0.6 --shared 0.8`; a few minutes each).
